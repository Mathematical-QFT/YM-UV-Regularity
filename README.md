# YM-UV-Regularity

Recent months have brought new developments at the interface of AI and mathematical quantum field theory. Ethan Sussman (Northwestern University) and Levent Alpöge (Anthropic) released an AI-generated manuscript reporting progress towards a construction of Yang–Mills theory in four-dimensional spacetime (YM₄). Shortly afterwards, OpenAI announced a construction of YM₄ but initially shared the proof only with a group of experts. On November 10, 2026, the group of experts released the Lean formalization and an assessment concluding that it establishes the existence of a four-dimensional Yang–Mills theory.

All this raises the question of to what extent constructing a realistic quantum field theory requires the know-how, internal models and computing power of Big Tech companies. This is a special case of a much broader concern. Will mathematical physicists in the AI era become mere consumers of knowledge generated elsewhere, stripped of their creative agency? Or will creativity and human understanding of mathematical structures not only survive but perhaps flourish at an entirely new level?

To find out, we set out to construct YM₄ using only broadly available AI tools. This repository contains our intermediate results. Our machine-generated manuscript ("slop") concerns the small-field contribution of Euclidean YM₄ in a fixed box. Its main result describes the behaviour of the coupling constant under the renormalization group flow, which exhibits asymptotic freedom. This is arguably one of the physically most interesting aspects of the YM₄ problem. We therefore decided to address it first, in a simplified setting, and to leave the large-field and infinite-volume problems as well as correlation functions for later work. To our knowledge, no complete exposition of this scope appeared before, perhaps with the exception of the OpenAI release of November 10, 2026. The first version presented here was created before this release. Our manuscript has 522 pages and comes with a 296-page Details volume that expands the most condensed computations.

We are aware that LLMs do not always credit their sources properly, so we cannot be sure that only the sources cited in our manuscripts were used. We did not use the manuscripts of Sussman and Alpöge as sources in this project. However, we studied them with Claude and ChatGPT before the project began, so we cannot exclude an indirect influence.

Our workflow was as follows. Using a Standard seat of the Claude Team plan for scientists and Claude Opus 5.5, we obtained the small-field flow of the couplings for YM₃ in a box in about four days of intensive prompting (26–29 October 2026). Claude was asked to use only Bałaban's approach, following his papers, and to stay close to the structure of Dimock's expositions of the φ⁴₃ case. Claude checked the manuscript itself in several verification passes. We then started upgrading the manuscript to YM₄, switching to a Premium seat halfway through. The upgrade took about four and a half days (29 October – 3 November) and was followed by an internal audit of the new four-dimensional parts. Meanwhile, the YM₃ manuscript went through three rounds of external audits with ChatGPT-6.1 Sol and GPT-6 Pro (Astra) (available under ChatGPT for Academic Researchers license), and the corrections were made in both manuscripts. To make sure no errors were hidden in the densest passages of the YM₄ manuscript, we asked Claude to write the Details volume, which spells out the steps omitted in the main text. This volume and its audits helped us find many mistakes. Four more external audits followed (5–10 November): one of the YM₃ manuscript and three of the YM₄ manuscript. (The audits were completed before the OpenAI release of November 10). Our audits found mistakes, but so far no fatal errors. We consider this version work in progress and intend to continue the audits.

Our experiment shows that fundamental problems, such as the existence of realistic QFTs, can be addressed by small groups of scientists equipped with broadly accessible AI tools. For now, we rely on AI audits for mathematical correctness of this manuscript. Instead, our emphasis is on the fact that, because we understand the techniques beforehand, we can extract well-structured and meaningful pieces which will constitute the construction of realistic QFTs, with the help of AI. Our recognition of such structures will also help organize autoformalization tools, which will eventually verify the correctness of the results. Moreover, we plan to use the flexibility of the AI workflow, not only to digest the Bałaban method, but to push it to its applicability, to look for simplifications and for signatures of interesting physical effects.

We can do it, and we can do it today, because we recognize the overall structure of the argument, which we learned, in particular, from Jonathan Dimock's outstanding papers on the Bałaban method. This is precisely why it matters to us to work with manuscripts that we can control with the tools at our disposal. We are scientists, not mere readers: exploration is the essence of our profession.
